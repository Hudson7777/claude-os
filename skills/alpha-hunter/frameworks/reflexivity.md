# Framework: Reflexivity — Self-Reinforcing Loops in Tech Themes

> "Every bubble has two components: an underlying trend that prevails in reality and a misconception relating to that trend. When a positive feedback develops between the trend and the misconception, a boom-bust process is set in motion."
> — George Soros

Reflexivity is when **the market's perception of a theme directly changes the theme's fundamentals**, creating a feedback loop. AI infrastructure is one of the most reflexive sectors in modern markets, and ignoring this dynamic leads to:
- Buying into the second-half of a self-reinforcing rally (and getting trapped at the top)
- Selling fundamentals-strong themes too early (because you don't realize the loop has more legs)
- Missing the precise moment the loop breaks

## The Two Principles

**Fallibility**: All market participants — including you — operate with imperfect understanding. The "fundamental value" you're computing is itself an estimate based on assumptions that other participants are also estimating. This is not a problem to solve; it's a condition to navigate.

**Reflexivity**: Market participants' beliefs influence the very fundamentals they're trying to assess. Examples:
- Rising stock price → easier to raise capital → more capex → higher revenue capacity → higher actual revenue → higher stock price (positive loop)
- Rising stock price → easier to attract talent / customers → competitive advantages compound → higher fundamentals → higher stock price
- Falling stock price → harder to raise capital → forced layoffs → competitive position deteriorates → fundamentals worsen → falling stock price (negative loop)

## Why AI Infrastructure Is Highly Reflexive

Tech infrastructure has unusually strong reflexivity for several reasons:

1. **Capex is the fundamental driver of revenue.** When NVIDIA's stock rises, hyperscalers see "AI is real" confirmed and raise their capex. NVIDIA's revenue then rises, validating the price. The loop is direct.

2. **Power-law winners.** When a category leader emerges (NVIDIA, TSMC, ASML), customers preferentially route demand to them, which validates the leadership and attracts more demand. Reflexivity compounds the leader's advantage.

3. **Capital availability sensitivity.** AI infra companies (especially smaller ones — neoclouds, specialty cooling, power) depend on capital markets to fund capex. Their stock price IS their growth lever.

4. **Narrative-validation cycles.** When new architectures (CoWoS, HBM, optical, liquid cooling) get attention, more research and investment flows in, which accelerates the architecture's actual progress. The narrative produces the reality.

5. **Hyperscaler capex feedback.** 5 largest US cloud and AI infrastructure companies committed $660-690 billion capex for 2026, ~2x 2025 levels, ~1.9% of GDP — capex of this magnitude itself becomes a macro variable, validating the theme it's funding.

## How to Identify a Reflexive Loop

For any theme, ask:

### 1. Is there a direct causal channel from price → fundamentals?
- Does rising stock price enable the company to do something operationally that improves fundamentals?
- Channels: capital raises (equity issuance), cheaper debt, employee retention via stock comp, customer trust, M&A optionality

If yes, the theme has reflexive potential. Catalog the channels explicitly.

### 2. What's the underlying trend (real)?
Soros's framework: every reflexive loop has a "real" trend underneath. For AI infra, the real trend is something like "AI workloads scale → infrastructure demand scales." This is fundamental and would exist with or without market enthusiasm.

### 3. What's the misconception (the part the market is exaggerating)?
The reflexive loop ALWAYS contains a misconception that gets amplified. Common misconceptions in AI infra:
- "Capex levels are sustainable indefinitely"
- "Single-vendor lock-in is permanent"
- "Margins will stay at peak levels through the cycle"
- "Customer concentration is not a risk"
- "There will be no competitive entry"

Identifying the misconception is critical because **the loop breaks when the misconception is exposed**.

### 4. Where in the loop are we?

Soros breaks reflexive cycles into stages:
- **Phase 1 (Latent)**: Trend forming, market unaware, no loop yet → this is **Stage 0 Alpha territory**
- **Phase 2 (Acceleration)**: Trend recognized, beliefs reinforce trend, fundamentals validate → **Stage 1-2; loop is operating in your favor**
- **Phase 3 (Test)**: Reality tests the misconception (a setback, a competitor, a slowdown). If the loop survives the test, the misconception is reinforced. If not, it breaks.
- **Phase 4 (Twilight)**: Loop continues but participants begin to doubt; "wall of worry" climbing
- **Phase 5 (Reversal)**: Misconception exposed, loop reverses, prices crash faster than fundamentals deteriorate

The hardest phase is Phase 4, because fundamentals are often still strong but the loop is fragile.

### 5. What would break the loop?
**Identify ahead of time the specific event(s) that would expose the misconception.** Examples:
- A hyperscaler publicly cutting capex guidance
- A technology obsoleting the current architecture (new memory paradigm, new compute paradigm)
- A regulatory event removing a key advantage
- A failed product cycle from the category leader
- Competition emerging from an unexpected source

## Practical Application: The Reflexivity Check

For every theme research, add a "reflexivity check":

1. **Reflexivity strength** (low / medium / high): How directly do prices feed back into fundamentals?
2. **Loop direction** (positive / negative): Currently self-reinforcing in which direction?
3. **Underlying trend** (the real part)
4. **Embedded misconception** (the exaggerated part)
5. **Loop phase** (1-5 above)
6. **Loop-breaker** (specific event that would expose the misconception)
7. **Implication for sizing**: a theme with high reflexivity in late-loop phases requires smaller positions and tighter stops; a theme in latent or acceleration phases can be sized larger.

## Why This Matters Especially Now

In the AI infrastructure cycle, reflexivity is at extreme levels. The hyperscaler capex → semiconductor revenue → semiconductor stock price → hyperscaler capex confidence loop is one of the most powerful feedback mechanisms in market history. This means:

- Themes can run far past "fundamental" valuation while the loop operates
- The reversal, when it comes, will be faster and more violent than fundamentals would suggest
- Late entries face the highest reflexivity risk

Conversely:
- Themes that are CURRENTLY in the latent phase (e.g., new bottlenecks not yet validated) have the most reflexive upside if the loop catches
- Themes in phase 4-5 should be exited or trimmed regardless of fundamental quality

## The Soros Question

Whenever you feel strong conviction about a theme, ask:
> **"What part of my 'fundamental' analysis is downstream of the price action I've already observed?"**

If a meaningful part of your conviction comes from price-validated narrative (capex, revenue, hiring all happening BECAUSE the theme is hot), you're inside a reflexive loop. That's not a reason to exit — but it is a reason to know what you're exposed to and to identify the loop-breaker explicitly.
