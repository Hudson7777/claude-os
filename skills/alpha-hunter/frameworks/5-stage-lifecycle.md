# Framework: 5-Stage Theme Lifecycle

Every Alpha theme passes through a relatively predictable sequence of awareness expansion. Identifying which stage a theme is currently in determines:
- Whether Alpha is still extractable
- What kind of evidence to look for
- How much portfolio risk is justified

## The 5 Stages

### Stage 0 — Technical Necessity (awareness <5%)

The technology / supply-chain bottleneck is visible only to insiders: domain engineers, academic researchers, primary OEM staff, specialty equity analysts who happen to cover the precise vertical.

**Indicators**:
- Topic appears in academic papers, conference proceedings, or specialty technical blogs
- Zero or near-zero financial media coverage
- No sell-side initiation reports
- CEO抱怨提取信号 just emerging — but not yet picked up by mainstream financial analysts

**Investability**: Highest theoretical Alpha, but technical-validation risk is also highest. Position-sizing should reflect uncertainty. This is where "deep technical reading" pays the most.

### Stage 1 — Industry Consensus Forming (awareness 5–15%)

Specialist research outlets begin publishing deep analyses. Supply-chain data starts showing anomalies. Top vendors mention the topic in earnings Q&A — but the topic is not yet the headline of the call.

**Indicators**:
- SemiAnalysis / The Information / similar specialty research has 1–2 deep dives
- TrendForce / DigiTimes show price or order anomalies
- 1–2 sell-side analysts have quietly upgraded category leaders
- Twitter/X specialist accounts (@dylan522p etc.) discussing actively
- Mainstream财经 / 雪球 still NOT focused on it

**Investability**: **Best risk/reward zone.** Technical uncertainty largely resolved, but market hasn't priced it in. This is where the bulk of conviction sizing should happen for a research-driven investor.

### Stage 2 — Smart Money Building (awareness 15–40%)

Specialist hedge funds and sector PMs are visibly building positions. Multiple sell-side initiations with measured tone. Stocks in the segment show structural uptrends with high volatility.

**Indicators**:
- 13F filings show specialist funds adding positions
- Multiple sell-side initiations (not just one)
- Theme has a clear name now (e.g., "AI power", "光互联")
- Stock prices in the segment trending up with 20–30% drawdowns along the way
- Mainstream财经 starting to mention it occasionally

**Investability**: Still positive Alpha, but volatility is high. Late entries face whipsaw risk. Existing positions should be added on drawdowns, not chased on rips.

### Stage 3 — Sell-Side Consensus (awareness 40–70%)

All major sell-side houses have it as overweight. Mutual funds notably overweight vs benchmark. Mainstream financial media doing weekly coverage.

**Indicators**:
- All major sell-side houses have published thematic reports
- Mutual fund overweight clearly visible in fund letters
- Bloomberg / CNBC / 华尔街见闻 doing weekly coverage
- Multiple expansion (P/E, P/S) into top quartile of historical range
- "AI XYZ" becomes a tradeable basket / ETF launches

**Investability**: Mostly Beta now, marginal Alpha. Position holdings should be **trimmed on strength**. New entries justified only with very specific stock-level edge, not theme-level edge.

### Stage 4 — Mass Awareness (awareness >70%)

The theme is in mainstream conversation. Retail crowding is visible. New entrants with weak fundamentals get extreme valuation premiums just for being in the category.

**Indicators**:
- Theme on 雪球 / Twitter retail / TikTok / 小红书
- IPO window opens for category-adjacent companies, often with weak fundamentals
- Multiple expansion above historical highs
- "This time is different" narrative
- 出租车司机 / friends-and-family discussion

**Investability**: **Reduce, don't add.** Top of cycle is statistically near. Even good companies are likely to underperform from this entry point. Existing positions should be reviewed for trim/exit. New positions should be avoided unless there is a very specific contrarian sub-thesis.

## Stage Transition Dynamics

- Stages 0→1: Driven by technical validation events (working prototypes, capacity announcements, first customer ships)
- Stages 1→2: Driven by financial validation (revenue inflection, margin proof, multiple specialty fund positions)
- Stages 2→3: Driven by sell-side initiation cascade
- Stages 3→4: Driven by retail flow and ETF inclusion

The fastest transitions historically are 1→2 (often <6 months) and 3→4 (often <3 months). The longest is typically 0→1 (can be 1–3 years).

## How long does each stage last?

Highly variable, but rough heuristics from historical AI infrastructure cases:
- Stage 0: 1–3 years (often invisible to all but specialists)
- Stage 1: 6–18 months (this is where Alpha is harvested)
- Stage 2: 6–12 months
- Stage 3: 6–12 months
- Stage 4: 3–9 months before correction or sustained plateau

## Cognitive failure modes by stage

- **Stage 0 trap**: "Too speculative" — passing on a real technical necessity because it lacks financial validation
- **Stage 1 trap**: "Already moved" — refusing to enter because the stocks are off the lows, missing the bulk of the move
- **Stage 2 trap**: Chasing on momentum without re-validating thesis; buying at local tops
- **Stage 3 trap**: Mistaking sell-side consensus for personal conviction; staying past peak
- **Stage 4 trap**: Believing "this time is different"; failing to trim into euphoria

## Cross-checking your stage assessment

Whenever you assess a theme as Stage X, also ask:
1. What would have to be true for me to be one stage too early? (i.e., this is actually X-1)
2. What would have to be true for me to be one stage too late? (i.e., this is actually X+1)
3. What does the historical analog from the post-mortems file suggest about typical stage durations for this kind of theme?

A confident stage assessment WITHOUT this cross-check is likely overconfident.
